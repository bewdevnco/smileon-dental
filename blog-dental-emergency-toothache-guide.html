What to Do During a Severe Toothache or Broken Tooth Emergency?

By Dr. Neha Sarda (Chief Dental Surgeon)
Updated Nov 14, 2026
3 min read
Clinically Reviewed
Immediate Emergency Protocol: Rinse your mouth gently with warm salt water. Apply a cold ice compress outside your cheek for 15 minutes. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever like ibuprofen or paracetamol. Never place aspirin directly against the gum tissue, as it causes severe chemical burns. 1. Severe Throbbing Toothache First Aid

An intense throbbing toothache that prevents sleep or radiates into the jaw, ear, or temple usually signals acute pulpitis (bacterial infection of the inner tooth nerve).

  • Warm Salt Water Rinse: Dissolve 1/2 teaspoon of salt in warm water. Swish gently to dislodge food particles and soothe tissues.
  • Dental Floss Check: Carefully run dental floss between adjacent teeth to ensure a hard food fragment (such as a popcorn hull or seed) is not wedged into the gum pocket.
  • Cold Compress: Hold an ice pack wrapped in a clean cloth against the cheek over the painful area for 15-minute intervals.
  • Avoid Extreme Temperatures: Do not consume ice water, hot tea, or sweet beverages that trigger extreme nerve reactions.

2. Chipped or Broken Tooth Protocol

Whether caused by biting hard food or sports trauma, prompt action saves the tooth's nerve and structural integrity:

  1. Save Any Broken Fragments: Rinse the broken piece in clean water and store it in a container of cold milk or saliva. In many instances, dentists can re-bond the natural fragment with composite resin!
  2. Cover Sharp Edges: If the jagged edge is cutting your tongue or cheek, place a small piece of sugarless chewing gum or orthodontic wax over the sharp point.
  3. Visit Same-Day: Leaving the inner dentin exposed allows oral bacteria to infect the nerve within 24–48 hours.

3. Knocked-Out (Avulsed) Permanent Tooth

A completely knocked-out tooth is one of the few true dental emergencies where time is critical (30–60 minute window):

  • Handle Only by the Crown: Never touch the root surfaces. The delicate periodontal ligament fibers on the root are living cells needed for reattachment.
  • Rinse Briefly: If dirty, rinse with milk or saline for 5 seconds. Do not scrub or dry with a towel.
  • Storage Medium: Store the tooth submerged in a cup of cold fresh milk or inside your own cheek pouch. Do NOT store it in plain tap water.
  • Rush to the Clinic: Arriving at SmileOn within 45 minutes gives our surgeons a 90% chance of successfully replanting and splinting the tooth back into your jawbone!

4. Facial Swelling & Dental Abscesses

A visible lump on the gum that resembles a pimple (fistula) or spreading facial swelling indicates an acute bacterial abscess. This requires urgent antibiotics and drainage. An untreated dental abscess can spread into the facial planes and neck spaces, becoming a dangerous medical condition.

Chief Dental Surgeon • SmileOn Dental Care

"Never ignore a sudden toothache that suddenly stops hurting on its own. Often, the cessation of pain means the nerve has died, and bacteria are now quietly destroying the jawbone beneath."
