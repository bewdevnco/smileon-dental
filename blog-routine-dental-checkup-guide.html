Preventive Dentistry

How Often Should You Visit the Dentist & Why Preventive Care Saves Money

By Dr. Neha Sarda (Chief Dentist)
Updated Nov 14, 2026
3 min read
Clinically Reviewed
The Short Verdict: Dental health organizations worldwide recommend visiting the dentist every 6 months. Enamel decay and gum disease rarely cause pain in their initial stages. A quick ₹500–₹1,500 checkup catches minor issues when a simple ₹1,200 composite filling solves them, rather than waiting for an emergency ₹8,000 root canal or ₹35,000 implant!

Why Is the 6-Month Rule So Important?

The human tooth does not have pain receptors inside its outer enamel layer. This means dental caries (cavities) develop in complete silence. By the time a tooth begins throbbing or throbs when drinking hot tea, the bacterial decay has already burrowed deep through the enamel and dentin into the living pulp tissue.

Visiting every 6 months allows our specialists to spot microscopic demineralization and treat it with fluoride remineralization or a tiny filling long before the nerve is jeopardized.

Preventive Care vs. Reactive Treatment Costs

Stage Clinical Condition Required Treatment Estimated Cost (INR)
Early (0–6 Months) Micro surface cavity or plaque Scaling or Small Filling ₹1,000 – ₹1,800
Moderate (1–2 Years Delayed) Deep decay reaching inner dentin Inlay/Onlay or Deep Restoration ₹2,500 – ₹4,500
Severe (Ignored Cavity) Pulp infection & severe toothache Root Canal + Zirconia Crown ₹9,000 – ₹15,000
Critical (Tooth Fractured/Lost) Non-restorable broken root Extraction + Titanium Implant ₹30,000 – ₹55,000

What Happens During a SmileOn Comprehensive Checkup?

  • Intraoral HD Camera Tour: See exactly what the doctor sees on high-definition chairside screens.
  • Low-Radiation Digital X-Rays: Pinpoints hidden decay between tight molar contact points.
  • Periodontal Gum Pocket Screening: Measures gum attachment depths to stop bone loss before teeth become loose.
  • Oral Cancer & Soft Tissue Exam: Screens tongue, cheeks, and palate for early lesions or abnormalities.
  • Bite & Wear Analysis: Identifies nighttime teeth grinding (bruxism) and enamel erosion.

Golden Rules for Daily At-Home Oral Hygiene

  1. Brush Twice Daily for 2 Full Minutes: Use a soft-bristled brush angled at 45 degrees towards the gumline.
  2. Floss Daily: 40% of tooth surfaces lie between teeth where toothbrush bristles can never enter.
  3. Replace Your Toothbrush Every 3 Months: Frayed bristles lose their cleaning power and harbor oral bacteria.
  4. Rinse After Meals: Swish with water after coffee, tea, and sugary treats to neutralize bacterial acids.
